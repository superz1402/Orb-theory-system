# Orb Theory — Pilot #1 (Short)
## The colour that was banned for killing people — Scheele's Green

**Format:** Vertical Short, 1080×1920, ~50 seconds
**Narrator:** The Orb (Orb Theory mascot)
**Tone:** Curiosity, lightly dark, no listicle

### Script (narration, ~122 words)

**[0–6s — HOOK]**
This colour killed people — and the Victorians put it on their walls anyway.

**[6–15s — THE INVENTION]**
I'm the Orb. In 1775, Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele invented a green so vivid, nothing else came close. The secret? Copper arsenite. Yes — arsenic.

**[15–25s — IT WENT EVERYWHERE]**
It went everywhere: wallpaper, dresses, candles, even green blancmange sweets.

**[25–37s — THE POISONING]**
But damp walls grew mould that turned the pigment into poisonous gas. Flakes fell as dust. Children wasted away in bright green bedrooms.

**[37–47s — MATILDA]**
In 1861, nineteen-year-old Matilda Scheueur died dusting artificial leaves with it — her eyes and fingernails turned green.

**[47–55s — PAYOFF + CTA]**
Some countries banned arsenic pigments. Britain just argued — and by the 1890s, the makers quietly stopped. Follow Orb Theory. Beauty can kill.

### Accuracy note
The working title says "banned" — the script itself is precise: some European nations banned arsenic-containing pigments in the 1830s–40s, Britain never formally banned its use in wallpaper, it fell out of favour after the 1860s and the last wallpaper brand using it ceased production by the 1890s.
